AI-Assisted Analysis and Problem Solving

How I approach prompts, review AI output, and connect new tools with analytical work.
Jason

From a question to a useful prompt#

I am expanding my programming and analytical toolkit with AI-assisted workflows. My focus is on making the task clear enough to evaluate the result: what decision needs support, what information is available, and what a useful answer should contain.

For an analytical task, I structure prompts around:

  1. Business context: The audience, the question, and the decision the analysis should inform.
  2. Available evidence: The schema, metric definitions, time period, and known data gaps.
  3. Constraints: Avoid unsupported assumptions, distinguish correlation from causation, and identify missing information.
  4. Deliverable: Request a reviewable query, a concise explanation, or a proposed next step with its limitations.

Review is part of the work#

Generated SQL, code, and summaries still require checks. I review joins, filters, aggregation levels, and date handling, and compare outputs with source data before drawing conclusions. A plausible explanation is a starting point for investigation.

For customer and operational work, the same discipline helps turn a vague request into clear requirements, an actionable checklist, or a draft that a colleague can review.

Continuing development#

I am building experience in prompt design, AI-assisted coding, and repeatable analytical workflows. My goal is to combine these tools with business judgment and technical understanding, while keeping private customer and company information out of public examples.
